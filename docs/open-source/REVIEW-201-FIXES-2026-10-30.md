## The render check (DOC-1, DOC-9, DOC-12), measured

The method renders each page with `pdftoppm -gray -r 50` and takes word boxes from `pdftotext -bbox`. It then
counts two things: dark pixels outside every word box ("unexplained ink"), and words whose box holds no dark pixel
("hidden").

- **Attack PDFs (reviewer repros):**
  - The tiling-pattern text leaves 0.00086 unexplained ink, against 0.00139 for a harmless ruled line. No threshold
    separates the attack from ordinary graphics.
  - White text and render mode `3 Tr` each give 6 hidden words; clean text gives 0.
- **Real PDFs on this body:** there are only two outside test directories, 11 pages with text in all. That is too
  small a sample to rely on.
  - 10 of the 11 pages carry unexplained ink, from graphics.
  - 19 of 640 words read as hidden, probably light-grey text.
- **Cost:** a median of 0.15 s per PDF.

**Decision.** A rule that withholds `complete` on any unexplained ink would make absence unprovable on nearly every
designed document. It is not adopted, and DOC-1 and DOC-12 stay open: patterned, Type3 and outlined text can still
be vouched for. They are the remaining high-severity gap in this column.

The hidden-text signal is precise on the attacks. The proposed DOC-9 fix: when every occurrence of a found phrase
lies in hidden words, report `found: None`. That fails safe, and the mission asks. It needs `pdftoppm` as a third
pinned companion, with its own hash, its license assessment and an oracle case, so it is a material change to the
acquired capability, and it waits for review.
