## What this shows, and what it does not

**Shown, at laboratory tier.** On GREG's canonical mission path, competence one mission formed persists on the body. A later, different mission reuses it.
- The founder is asked nothing for the same family, and half as much across a family boundary.
- Nothing is re-qualified.
- Answers stay certified and correct, and the founder still attached everything that was attached.
- The negative control and the contamination check rule out the obvious ways the measurement could be wrong.

This is the founder's learning test (directive 2026-10-28, §11) measured as a pre-registered experiment: replay, same family and cross family (§9C), plus an unrelated control. Before this run, step 15 of the fifteen-step mission showed one instance of same-family reuse.

**Not shown:**
1. **No statistical learning.** No parameter was fitted and no routing weight changed. This is capability reuse through a governed registry. The direction of the effect follows from the mechanism, so the experiment's information lies in its integrity checks and in the cross-family result, not in a surprise.
2. **Not a total-cost win per pair.** Each experienced body paid for its construction mission once (work 6), then ran one task. Per pair, the totals are 6 against 6 on the same family and 9 against 6 across families. The gain is in the **marginal** cost of every *later* mission: k later same-family missions cost 0 asks instead of 2k.
3. **Not real-world.** The problems are synthetic, small (2 to 5 elements per side), and the founder key is a test key. There is no founder device, no real staffing or matching outcome, and no external verification.
4. **Not an ablation inside one body.** The control arm is the subtraction test: a fresh body without the earlier mission. A body that formed the competence and then detached it before the task was not run.
5. **Not a re-attach probe.** Attach asks were answered by a script that attaches exactly the VERIFIED capabilities each ask names. A real founder may decline, which would leave the experienced body without the competence.

## Negative and disclosed items

- **Development before the declaration:** two n=1 runs in namespace `greg-transfer-dev`. In the first, the contamination check was vacuous: it read no qualification seeds. That was fixed before the declaration. While writing the unit tests, the negative-control rule was also tightened: a negative control must show **no** task better, where it had allowed one in twelve. The declaration's §6 records both.
- **The exposure ledger does not index this harness:** `scripts/data_exposure_ledger.py --check` rebuilds byte-identically. The instances come from a string-seeded namespace, which the ledger has no record type for. **Namespace `greg-transfer-2026-10-28` is spent.** Any further transfer run needs a new namespace and its own declaration.
