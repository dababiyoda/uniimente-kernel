because governing state C is broken."*

services)**, the honest instantiation is:

That is a real transaction. **It is not one UNIIMENTE controls any governing state
in.** PR #53's preserved dissent already says so, and it is correct:

> *"C9 is not a venture, and every one of its nine conditions would be satisfied

Spider-Web reaches the same verdict from the other direction: **C9 has no control
point, so it cannot be a web.** It maps to none of the four super-nodes. Under step 7
— *"discard mechanisms mapping to none"* — the whole candidate discards as an
architecture.

That is not an argument against doing C9. It is an argument against calling it one
thing when it is another.

## The single most valuable finding

Side 3 and side 4 are the whole architecture, and they are the two nobody has tested.

The kernel has spent its entire existence building sides 2, 5 and 8 — control point,
eligibility, governance — to a standard well beyond its stage. Those are the sides an
engineer can build alone. Sides 3, 4, 6 and 7 require **someone else to accept
something**, and no amount of further building produces one.

> **The institution's proof layer is ahead of its market contact by roughly its
> entire history.** Continuing to build the proof layer is the highest-quality way
> to make no progress available.

This is the same conclusion the backcast reached about permission, arrived at
independently through market architecture. Two different skills, two different
methods, one finding.

## Cheapest decisive test

PR #53 already names it and it costs nothing to run:

> *"if the founder already holds a warm compliance or risk buyer for C1, C1 should be
> selected and this recommendation discarded without ceremony."*

**One question to the founder.** If yes, C1 becomes the candidate and side 3 gets its
first real test. If no, C9 proceeds as an instrument — explicitly labelled as
producing a CVO and no architecture — while C1's buyer question stays open as the
real strategic gate.

Second test, also cheap: draft the governance receipt a compliance buyer would need,
and show it to one such person without selling anything. That tests side 3 directly
for the price of a conversation.

## Decision

**Validate.**

- **Next external action:** ask the founder the PR #53 warm-buyer question, and show one drafted governance receipt to one compliance or risk professional.
- **External number it must change:** external parties who have inspected a UNIIMENTE governance receipt, **0 → 1**.
- **Evidence required:** a named person outside the institution stating whether the receipt would change a decision they actually make.
- **Deadline:** 30 days from founder ratification (N0 in the backcast).
- **Result that triggers termination:** three qualified reviewers say the receipt would change no decision they make. At that point side 4 is empty by evidence rather than by absence, C1 is dead, and the institution is a governance framework rather than a market participant — which is a legitimate thing to be, and should then be said plainly.

**Not `Commit`** — eight fatal gates absent. **Not `Kill`** — sides 1, 2 and 8 are
real and the decisive test is one conversation. **Not `Park`** — parking is for
designs waiting on something expensive; this waits on a question.

## Preserved dissent

**Against this audit's own framing.** Scoring C1's eight sides at all confers
structure on something with no buyer. The scores are estimates about a market nobody
has touched, and PR #53's second dissent — *"numeric scores confer an appearance of
rigour the evidence does not support"* — applies to this table exactly as it applied
to that one. The table is a structured argument. It is not a measurement, and sides 4,
6 and 7 scoring zero is the only part of it that is a fact.

**Against the recommendation.** A reasonable reading says run C9 immediately and
ignore C1 entirely until a CVO exists, on the grounds that an institution with zero
outcomes has no standing to design market architecture. That reading is strong. It is
not adopted only because the C1 test costs one conversation and can run in parallel
with C9 without competing for the active node.
